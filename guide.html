Pod Scanner Guide

Photographing whole bean pods, sharp from end to end, with the motorised microscope rig. One side takes about 5 minutes.

Page: smartestjamie-art.github.io/orius-counter/eggs.html
Use Chrome or Edge (on Mac or Windows). Safari and Firefox cannot talk to the rig.

1. Start of the day

  1. Plug the rig's USB cable and the microscope's USB cable into the computer. Switch on the 12 V power.
  2. Open the page in Chrome or Edge.
  3. Press Use camera (top right). The live microscope picture appears.
  4. Press Connect stage. The first time on a computer, pick the Arduino from the list.
  5. Wait about 40 s: the rig finds its three limit switches by itself (Z up, then Y, then X) and parks. The status line then ends in homed.
  6. If instead it says where the stage is is not known: press each of the three limit switches by hand. Each press should light the switch's small LED and show switch X, switch Y or switch Z on the status line. Only then press Home (under Adjust by hand). If a switch shows nothing, fix its plug first: homing without it can drive that slide into its end.

2. Scanning one pod

  1. Type the pod's name in Pod ID (for example Pod 12).
  2. Lay the pod along the rails, roughly under the lens and not too far forward. Then move the stage (arrow keys, or the X / Y buttons under Adjust by hand) until the pod's right end is in the middle of the live picture. The scan starts from wherever the stage is.
  3. Press Scan this side. The rig moves along the pod, takes a focus-stacked picture every 24 mm, and stops at the pod's tail. The stitched pod grows underneath the live picture as it goes.
  4. When it says Flip the pod: turn the pod over, move the stage so its right end is in the middle of the picture again, press Side B next, then Scan this side.
  5. If it says the pod lies too far forward (or back), move the pod the way it says and scan that side again.
  6. Press Next pod. This saves the pod first (one .zip in your Downloads folder), then moves on to the next pod's name.

The first scan after opening the page starts with a short calibration (the stage moves 8 mm in X and Y and back). That is normal.

3. Saving

  • Each pod is saved as one file: Pod_12_YYYYMMDD.zip, in your Downloads folder. It holds every shot as taken plus the stitched picture of each side.
  • Next pod saves automatically. Save this pod's photos saves at any time.
  • The line under the buttons says whether the pod is saved yet.
  • Copy the day's .zip files to the shared drive before you leave. Photos also stay in this browser, but only on this computer.

4. If something goes wrong

Something is about to hit, or the rig makes a bad noisePress Stop. Everything stops at once. Then press Home (under Adjust by hand) before scanning again.
“A limit signal flickered…”Nothing to do: it finds its switches again and carries on with the scan by itself.
“A limit switch was hit: backing off…”It moves off the switch by itself. Check nothing is in the way, then press Home and scan again. The scan carries on from the missing shots.
The live picture is black or frozenPress Use camera again. If the wrong camera shows, choose Digital Microscope under Adjust by hand.
“Not connected” / Connect does nothingCheck the USB cable and the 12 V power, reload the page, press Connect stage again. Close any other tab that has the page open.
The scan stopped early, before the tailPress Scan this side again: it continues from the next shot.
The pictures are blurred everywhereThe microscope's height may have changed (rig moved or knocked). Tell Jamie: the focus band needs measuring again.

5. Please don't

  • Don't turn the microscope's own focus or zoom ring. It is set and taped.
  • Don't push the slides by hand while the 12 V is on, and don't put anything behind the tray (the column is at the back).
  • Don't change anything under Rig settings.
  • Don't open the page in two tabs at once.

6. End of the day

  1. Make sure the last pod is saved (press Save this pod's photos if the line says it is not).
  2. Copy the .zip files to the shared drive.
  3. Switch off the 12 V, then unplug the USB.
